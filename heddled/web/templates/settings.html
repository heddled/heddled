Jarvis

Off, and it should stay off unless you want it. Jarvis is the opposite of everything else here: you talk to it and it writes its own tools, writes its own agents and tries them, in a tree of its own that nothing else can see. You answer every turn, so it stops when you stop — what it cannot spend past is the conversation's budget. It is not how you build something you intend to rely on; it is a way to find out what a goal would take.

{% for m in known_models %}

Jarvis can put its own agents on a schedule, which means they run with nobody watching. They share this budget between them, and when it is gone nothing of Jarvis's fires again until the next day. Set it to 0 and none of its schedules ever run. A scheduled agent still lives in Jarvis's tree and still holds none of your keys.

A step is one action and the thinking around it. Low, and it checks in often and you steer; high, and it works for longer and hands you more at once. When it runs out it stops and says so — that is not an error, and "carry on" picks it up where it left off.

What it can and cannot reach. It writes only into jarvis/ — your own agents/ and tools/ are a different tree it holds no path to. It can put its own agents on a schedule, which run unattended in that tree against the daily budget above and never in yours. It may ask your agents, on the jarvis channel, and their policies and approvals still apply; it can never change them. It holds none of your credentials — not in the Python it writes, which runs in a child process with no keys and no store, and not in the no-code tools either, which are handed your settings with every key removed. Nothing it makes is yours until you say so, and discarding a conversation takes everything it made with it. It keeps notes as Markdown in jarvis/memory/, which you can read and delete beside the conversation.
